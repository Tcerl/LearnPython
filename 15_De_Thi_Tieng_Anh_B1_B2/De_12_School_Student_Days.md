> ### Should Teenagers Have a Part-Time Job?
>
> In many countries, having a Saturday or holiday job is a normal part of growing up (discussed in Complete PET Unit 2, p.22). In the UK, for example, teenagers can legally do light part-time work – such as delivering newspapers, washing cars or helping in a shop – from the age of thirteen, provided they work no more than twelve hours a week during school term time. However, attitudes towards teenage work vary greatly around the world.
>
> Linh, a seventeen-year-old student from Vietnam, has never had a part-time job, and neither have most of her high-school classmates. 'In my country, high school is very demanding,' Linh explains. 'We have lessons in the morning and extra classes in the afternoon, plus lots of homework in the evening. My parents want me to concentrate 100% on passing my university entrance exams. Instead of working in a café, I help my mother cook dinner and clean the house every day. I think I will look for a part-time job as a tutor once I am at university.'
>
> By contrast, sixteen-year-old Marcelo from Colombia works for four hours every Saturday morning in his uncle's bakery. He gets up at 6 am to help arrange fresh bread and cakes on the shelves and serve early customers. 'I really enjoy my Saturday job,' says Marcelo. 'Because it is only four hours at the weekend, it doesn't affect my homework at all. In fact, earning my own money has taught me the value of hard work. I save half of what I earn for a new laptop for university, and I use the rest to go to the cinema with my friends. Serving customers has also made me much more confident and polite when speaking to adults.'
>
> Education experts say that both approaches have advantages. While a few hours of weekend work can teach teenagers time management, responsibility and communication skills, working too much is a mistake. Studies show that students who work more than fifteen hours a week during the school year often feel tired in class and get lower marks in exams. The key, it seems, is finding the right balance.

### 1. Bài nói mẫu Speaking Part 2 (SCHOOL & STUDENT DAYS):

I would like to talk about my favourite subject, which is English. I first started learning English when I was in third grade at primary school, so I have been studying it for more than ten years now.

In the past, I used to find English quite difficult because we only memorised grammar rules and long lists of vocabulary. However, my current teacher at university makes the lessons really lively and interesting. She is very patient and always encourages us not to be afraid of making mistakes.

During our lessons, we rarely sit in silence. Instead, we often work in pairs or small groups to discuss everyday topics, listen to short recordings, and play vocabulary games. Sometimes we also watch short English videos and practise pronunciation.

English is extremely important to me for several reasons. Firstly, passing the B1 English exam is a requirement for my graduation. More importantly, having good English skills will help me find a well-paid job in the future and talk confidently with people from all over the world.
