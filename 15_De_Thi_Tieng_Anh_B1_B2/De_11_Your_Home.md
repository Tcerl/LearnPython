> ### Swapping Homes for the Holidays
>
> Last August, Martin and Helen Clarke and their teenage twins spent two wonderful weeks in a spacious three-bedroom apartment in the centre of Barcelona, Spain. Their flat had a sunny roof terrace, a modern kitchen and a living room filled with books and games. Best of all, they didn't pay a single penny for their accommodation. At the exact same time, the owners of the Barcelona flat – Carlos, Elena and their two children – were staying in the Clarkes' cottage in Devon, England.
>
> The two families arranged their holiday through a home-swapping website. Although online home exchanges have grown rapidly in recent years, the idea actually began in the 1950s, when teachers in Europe and North America started swapping houses during the long summer holidays using printed directories. Today, homeowners pay an annual membership fee of around £100 to list photos and descriptions of their property on a website, and can then contact thousands of other members around the world.
>
> 'I must admit I was nervous before our first swap,' says Helen Clarke. 'I worried that strangers might break our plates or make a mess on the living room carpet. However, you soon realise that the other family is trusting you with their home too, so everyone treats each other's property with great respect.' Before finalising the arrangement, the two families had several video calls to show each other around their homes and ask questions.
>
> For the Clarkes, staying in a real family home was much better than booking two cramped hotel rooms. 'Having a proper kitchen meant we could buy fresh fish and fruit at the local market and cook breakfast and dinner ourselves, which saved us hundreds of pounds,' explains Martin. 'Carlos and Elena even left us their bicycles and metro passes, along with a handwritten list of their favourite neighbourhood bakeries and quiet beaches that tourists never find.'
>
> When the Clarkes returned to Devon, their cottage was spotless, and there was a bottle of Spanish olive oil and a thank-you card waiting on the kitchen table. The Clarkes are already planning their next exchange for Easter – this time with a family in Vancouver, Canada!

> ### Tidy Room, Tidy Mind?
>
> For years, my mother and I had the same argument every Saturday morning. She would open my bedroom door, look at the clothes on the armchair, the books piled on the floor and the empty mugs on my desk, and sigh deeply. 'How can you study in this mess?' she would ask. I always replied that I knew exactly where everything was, and that tidying up was a waste of valuable time.
>
> However, that excuse stopped working last November when I lost my history project the night before I had to hand it in. I spent three hours turning my bedroom upside down in a panic, only to find the pages crumpled underneath a pile of dirty sports kit at 1 am. The next day, exhausted and embarrassed, I decided that my mother might be right after all.
>
> Instead of trying to clean the whole room at once, I followed a simple rule I read in a magazine: the 'five-minute evening reset'. Every night before brushing my teeth, I set a timer on my phone for five minutes. In that short time, I put dirty clothes in the laundry basket, return books to the shelf and clear my desk for the next day.
>
> To my surprise, keeping my room tidy hasn't made me boring – it has actually given me more free time. I no longer spend ten minutes every morning searching for my keys or a matching pair of socks, and sitting down at a clear desk makes it much easier to concentrate on my homework.

## C. ĐÁP ÁN CHI TIẾT & BÀI NÓI MẪU – ĐỀ SỐ 11

### 1. Bài nói mẫu Speaking Part 2 (YOUR HOME):

I would like to talk about the house I live in with my parents and my younger sister. It is a cosy three-storey house located in a quiet residential street, about fifteen minutes by bus from the city centre. The outside of the house is painted cream and there is a small balcony with lots of colourful flowers on the second floor.

My favourite room in the house is definitely my bedroom on the second floor. It is not very large, but it gets plenty of sunshine in the morning. Inside, there is a comfortable bed with soft cushions, a wooden chest of drawers, a wardrobe, and a study desk right next to the window looking out over our small garden.

When I am at home in my free time, I usually sit at my desk to read books, listen to music, or chat online with my friends. In the evening, I often help my mother prepare dinner in the kitchen and then we watch television together in the living room.

I really love living in my house because it is a peaceful, warm place where I can completely relax after a long day at school.

Từ vựng & Cấu trúc ăn điểm:

- cosy (adj): ấm cúng (Complete PET Unit 1, p.11-12)

- balcony / corridor / stairs (n): ban công / hành lang / cầu thang (Complete PET Unit 1, p.13)

- chest of drawers / cushions (n): tủ ngăn kéo / gối tựa (Complete PET Unit 1, p.13)

- furniture (n - uncountable): đồ nội thất - danh từ không đếm được (Complete PET Unit 1, p.13)
