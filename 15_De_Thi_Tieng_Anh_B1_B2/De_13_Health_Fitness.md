> ### Why Teenagers Need More Sleep
>
> If you find it almost impossible to get out of bed at 7 am on a school morning, you are not necessarily lazy! According to sleep scientists, teenagers actually need between eight-and-a-half and nine-and-a-half hours of sleep every night – more than adults – because their bodies and brains are growing so rapidly.
>
> Research by Professor Russell Foster at Oxford University shows that during adolescence, a teenager's internal body clock naturally shifts forward by about two hours. The hormone melatonin, which makes us feel sleepy, is released much later at night in teenagers than in children or adults. This makes it biologically difficult for most teenagers to fall asleep before 11 pm, which means waking up at 6.30 am for school leaves them constantly exhausted.
>
> Modern technology makes the problem even worse. Many teenagers lie in bed texting friends, watching videos or playing games until midnight. The bright blue light from phones and laptops tricks the brain into thinking it is still daytime, stopping the body from preparing for sleep.
>
> To test whether later school hours would help, Monkseaton High School in northern England carried out a famous experiment by moving the start of the school day from 8.50 am to 10.00 am. The results were remarkable. Absence rates and lateness dropped significantly because fewer students overslept or caught colds. Teachers reported that pupils were much more alert, cheerful and ready to learn during the first lessons of the day. Even more impressively, the percentage of students passing their national exams in English and maths rose by 19%.
>
> Although many schools find it difficult to make the change because of bus timetables and parents' working hours, sleep experts say teenagers can still help themselves. Dr Foster advises teenagers to switch off all screens at least an hour before bedtime, charge their phones in another room overnight, and avoid coffee, tea or cola drinks after 4 pm.

### 1. Bài nói mẫu Speaking Part 2 (HEALTH & FITNESS):

I would like to talk about a healthy habit that I have maintained for the past two years to stay fit, which is morning jogging combined with drinking plenty of water.

Every morning from Monday to Saturday, I wake up at 5.45 am and go jogging for about thirty minutes in the public park near my house. At that time of the morning, the air is fresh, the streets are quiet, and there is very little traffic.

The great thing about jogging is that you don't need any expensive equipment. All I need is a good pair of comfortable trainers to protect my knees and ankles from injury, some light cotton sportswear, and a small bottle of water. Of course, it also requires self-discipline, especially on cold winter mornings.

This simple habit has improved my health dramatically. In the past, I used to feel exhausted in afternoon classes and often caught colds or flu when the weather changed. Since I started jogging regularly, I sleep much better at night and feel full of energy throughout the day.
