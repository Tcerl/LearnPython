> ### A Week Without Smartphones
>
> Could you survive for a whole week without your smartphone? Last month, sixty-five of the two hundred fifteen-year-old students at Oakridge Secondary School volunteered to take part in a 'Digital Detox' challenge organised by their science teachers.
>
> Before the experiment began, the students checked their screen-time apps and were shocked to discover that they were spending an average of five hours a day on their phones – mostly scrolling through social media, watching short videos and sending hundreds of group messages. The volunteers handed in their smartphones to the headteacher's office on Monday morning, where the devices were locked in a cupboard for seven days.
>
> The first forty-eight hours were the hardest. Many students admitted feeling restless and anxious, constantly reaching into their pockets for a phone that wasn't there. 'I overslept and missed the school bus on Tuesday morning because I usually use my phone as an alarm clock!' laughed fifteen-year-old Olivia Grant. 'After that, I had to borrow my dad's old-fashioned alarm clock and a paper street map.'
>
> By Thursday, however, the atmosphere in the school canteen had transformed. Instead of sitting in silence staring at screens, students were playing card games, telling jokes and having lively conversations. Even better, the volunteers noticed a huge improvement in their concentration at home. Without constant message notifications interrupting them every two minutes, they finished their homework in half the usual time.
>
> Parents were delighted too, reporting that their teenagers stayed at the dinner table longer, helped with cooking, and went to bed an hour earlier than usual. Although everyone was happy to get their phone back the following Monday, most students decided to change their habits. 'The challenge made me realise that I was controlling my phone less than it was controlling me,' says Olivia. 'Now I turn off all notifications after 7 pm and leave my phone downstairs when I go to bed.'

## C. ĐÁP ÁN CHI TIẾT & BÀI NÓI MẪU – ĐỀ SỐ 15

### 1. Bài nói mẫu Speaking Part 2 (COMMUNICATION & TECHNOLOGY):

I would like to talk about how I communicate with my family and friends in daily life. Nowadays, thanks to smartphones and the Internet, I mostly use instant messaging apps like Zalo and Messenger, as well as video calls. For formal communication with my university lecturers, I usually send emails.

Every single day, I exchange text messages with my classmates about our group assignments and chat with my best friend. In the evening, I always make a ten-minute video call to my parents so we can see each other's faces and talk about our day.

Although texting is quick and convenient, I definitely prefer talking face-to-face when I need to discuss something serious, share personal feelings, or solve a misunderstanding. When you talk in person, you can hear the other person's tone of voice and see their facial expressions.

Overall, modern technology has changed communication dramatically. It allows us to keep in touch with relatives living thousands of kilometres away instantly and free of charge, as long as we remember to put our phones down when we are sitting together.

Từ vựng & Cấu trúc ăn điểm:

- keep / stay in touch with (phr): giữ liên lạc với ai (Complete PET Unit 5, p.47)

- instant messaging apps (n): các ứng dụng nhắn tin tức thời

- talk face-to-face / in person (phr): trò chuyện trực tiếp ngoài đời

- facial expressions / tone of voice (n): biểu cảm khuôn mặt / tông giọng

- free of charge (phr): hoàn toàn miễn phí
