> ### Parkrun: The Free Saturday Morning Run
>
> Every Saturday morning at 9 am, hundreds of thousands of people in over twenty countries tie up their running shoes and gather in local parks. They are taking part in Parkrun, a weekly five-kilometre timed event that is open to everyone of all ages and abilities – from fast club athletes to parents pushing babies in prams and people who prefer to walk.
>
> Parkrun began in October 2004 in Bushy Park, London, when a South African runner named Paul Sinton-Hewitt organised a short time trial for just 13 runners and a handful of friends. At the time, Paul was injured and couldn't train properly, but he still wanted a way to spend time with his running friends on Saturday mornings, followed by a cup of coffee.
>
> Word spread quickly, and soon other parks in the UK and abroad asked if they could set up their own events. Every event follows the same simple format: a five-kilometre course in a local park on Saturday morning at 9 am. Taking part is completely free. You simply register once on the website, print out a personal barcode, and bring it with you so volunteers can scan it at the finish line to record your time. Later that afternoon, you receive an email with your result.
>
> The events are organised entirely by unpaid volunteers who set up the course, cheer the runners on, and operate the stopwatches. Because Parkrun is not a race, slower joggers and walkers are just as welcome as fast runners. Every week, there is even a designated 'Tail Walker' who stays right at the back, making sure that no participant ever has to worry about coming last.
>
> Today, health experts praise Parkrun for helping people become more active and less lonely. In fact, hundreds of family doctors across the UK now prescribe Parkrun to patients suffering from stress, loneliness or heart problems. After crossing the finish line, most participants head to a nearby café to chat over tea and cake – proving that the social side is just as important as the exercise.

### 1. Bài nói mẫu Speaking Part 2 (SPORTS):

I would like to talk about my favourite sport, which is badminton. It is one of the most popular sports in Vietnam because it is easy to learn and does not require expensive equipment – just two rackets and a shuttlecock.

I have been playing badminton for about four years, ever since I started tenth grade in high school. At first, my father taught me the basic rules and how to serve properly, and I quickly became keen on it.

Nowadays, I usually play badminton three times a week after class or after work. I often play doubles with my close friends or my younger brother at a community sports court near my house. Sometimes we even have friendly matches against our neighbours.

Playing sports is extremely important to me for several reasons. First of all, it helps me stay fit, build stamina, and sleep better at night. Secondly, after sitting at a desk for hours studying, running around the court allows me to clear my mind and forget all my worries.

Từ vựng & Cấu trúc ăn điểm:

- equipment (n - uncountable): trang thiết bị, dụng cụ

- be keen on (adj): yêu thích, hào hứng với
