> ### Watching Wildlife in India
>
> Located in central India, Kanha National Park is one of the finest places in Asia to see wild tigers and inspired the famous stories of The Jungle Book (featured in Complete PET Unit 11, p.99). Last winter, twenty-one-year-old student Lucy Green spent a week there on a wildlife photography holiday.
>
> 'We stayed in a small eco-lodge right on the edge of the park,' Lucy recalls. 'The beds were very comfortable, and my room had a wonderful balcony overlooking the river and the forest trees, where colourful birds gathered every morning.'
>
> Every morning at 5.30 am, small groups of visitors set off into the forest in open four-wheel-drive jeeps, accompanied by an experienced local guide. Because the park covers nearly 1,000 square kilometres of thick bamboo forest and grassland, spotting a tiger is not easy. On her first two days, Lucy saw plenty of deer, monkeys and peacocks, but no tigers.
>
> On the third morning, however, their luck changed. 'Our guide suddenly stopped the engine and asked us to be completely silent,' says Lucy. 'He hadn't seen the tiger yet, but he had heard monkeys and deer making loud warning calls in the trees, which meant a predator was walking nearby.' Ten minutes later, a huge male tiger stepped out of the tall grass. When they first saw it, it was resting quietly in the shade by a stream after a meal, completely ignoring the cameras clicking in the jeep.
>
> For safety reasons, tourists are strictly forbidden to get out of the vehicle or feed the monkeys and birds, though they are allowed to take as many photographs as they like without using a flash. Apart from tigers, the park is home to leopards, sloth bears, rare swamp deer and over 300 species of birds.
>
> The park is open from October to June, and closes from July to September during the heavy monsoon rains when the dirt tracks become impossible to drive on. The entrance fees paid by visitors are used to employ forest guards and protect the wildlife from illegal hunting. 'Waking up at 5 am in the freezing cold was hard,' says Lucy, 'but seeing a wild tiger just ten metres away made every second worth it!'

## C. ĐÁP ÁN CHI TIẾT & BÀI NÓI MẪU – ĐỀ SỐ 14

### 1. Bài nói mẫu Speaking Part 2 (NATURE & ENVIRONMENT):

I would like to talk about the everyday actions I take to help protect the environment. Even though I am just one person, I believe small daily habits can make a big difference.

First of all, whenever I go to the supermarket or local market, I always bring my own reusable cloth bag instead of taking single-use plastic bags. I also carry a refillable metal water bottle to university every day. At home, I make sure to switch off the lights, fans, and air-conditioning before leaving my room, and I separate glass bottles, paper, and plastic tins for recycling.

I started doing these things seriously about two years ago after watching a television documentary about how plastic waste is harming sea turtles and fish in the ocean. At first, I sometimes forgot my cloth bag, but now it has become a natural habit and is not difficult at all.

Protecting the environment is extremely important to me because climate change and air pollution affect everyone's health. If we all conserve energy and reduce waste, we can keep our planet green and safe for future generations.

Từ vựng & Cấu trúc ăn điểm:

- energy conservation (n): việc tiết kiệm năng lượng (Complete PET Unit 11, p.101)

- reusable cloth bag (n): túi vải tái sử dụng nhiều lần

- single-use plastic (n): nhựa dùng một lần

- switch off the lights (phr): tắt đèn (Complete PET Unit 11, p.101)

- make a difference (phr): tạo nên sự khác biệt (Complete PET Unit 11, p.101)
